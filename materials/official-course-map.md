## Corrections to the course block (said in the lead's report)

1. Eisenhower's Evanston address was on **19 August 1954**, not 4 August.
2. The interruption number in Mark's papers is **25 min 26 s** to resume (2005), not 23 min 15 s, which is untraced.
3. "40 percent" is not a result of Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans (2001); it is Meyer's estimate quoted by APA in 2006 (reported).
4. The block's ladder rung 1 ("book every hour") and its anti-lever ("book to 100 percent") were the same setting. The anti-lever became **"say yes to every ask"**: no intake cap at all, which on rung 1 means 115% of focus hours by estimate.
5. Warm contrast darkened from mint `#2E7D6B` to `#2A7362` so mint text clears 4.5:1 on its own 50-tint (4.30 -> 4.92).

## The disagreements to teach

- **Who said urgent vs important** (session 1): Eisenhower quoted an unnamed college president in 1954; the matrix is Covey's, decades later. Teach both, never "Eisenhower's matrix" as fact.
- **What interruptions cost** (session 5): lab switch costs are tenths of a second per switch (F13, F14); the 40 percent is an estimate quoted by APA (F15); field observation shows interrupted work resumed after 25 minutes on average with huge spread (F11); and in the lab people got faster under interruption but more stressed (F10). These measure different things. Never resolve into one number.
- **Fixing estimates** (session 2): the past-experience prompt fixed the bias in Study 4 but not the finish date and not the accuracy (F4). Outside view helps calibration, not speed.
- **Parkinson's law** (session 3): satire about bureaucracy (reported), not evidence that slack gets wasted.

## Bench canon (session 4) - the calendar bench, 8 weeks, MODELLED

Engine `assets/tm-live.js`; reference `materials/tm_reference.py`; runner `materials/tm-node.js`.
Python and node agree on all 229 canon values exactly (diff prints nothing). Seed 20261010.

Defaults: 40 working days, 6 focus hours (360 min) a day. Requests arrive on a seeded stream with
a mean gap of 125 working minutes: data pull 40% (estimate 30/45/60/90 min, due next day), dashboard
fix 30% (60/90/120/180, due in 2 days), analysis 15% (240/360/480, due in 5 days), review 15% (30/60,
due next day). True time = estimate x a ratio drawn from 20 equally likely values
(80, 85, 90, 95, 100, 100, 105, 105, 105, 110, 110, 110, 115, 115, 120, 120, 125, 130, 140, 165 percent):
30% at or under estimate, matching Buehler Study 1's 29.7% (F1), with a far milder tail than the
thesis study's 55.5 / 33.9 because these are small tasks (said on the page). Quick asks of 5, 5 or
10 minutes about every 120 working minutes (132 in 8 weeks, 14.4 h), interrupting at once; resume
overhead 10 minutes (modelled assumption, slider 0-20). Focus block: the first 180 focus minutes;
quick asks held to one batch. Overtime up to 2 h a day on work due today or overdue. Intake: weekly
cap by ESTIMATE, ignoring carry-over. Order: earliest deadline first.

This draw: 128 requests offered, 270 h by estimate (112.5% of the 240 focus hours), 322 h true
(overrun 1.19 overall); 33 of 128 (25.8%) at or under estimate.

**Headline:** planning to 70 percent of focus hours by estimate became a real load of 93.8 percent,
because overruns and interruptions eat most of the slack. Slack is where the overrun goes.

**Two-number rung (2 vs 1):** rung 1 and rung 2 both finish 101 tasks inside the 8 weeks; rung 1 takes

## Per-session coverage

1. **Busy is not the same as important** - urgent vs important (F16, F17 tiered), one priority list, the inventory of commitments, the Dara case; stress as load against capacity introduced (F20 read); workload-not-medical line. Build-along: one list, sorted by consequence and date.
2. **Estimates are optimistic by default** - planning fallacy (F1-F6), best/worst case does not fix it (F2), the outside view and its limits (F4), observers (F5), Hofstadter (F22 reported). Build-along: re-estimate three tasks from your own past.
3. **Slack is not waste** - utilisation, the queueing curve (F7, F8, F9), why a full calendar makes everything late, buffers placed where variance is, Parkinson (F21 reported, satire). Build-along: compute your real load.
4. **The calendar bench** - canon above; ladder, two-number rung, anti-lever, break, Kingman check.
5. **Interruptions and the cost of switching** - F10-F15 with the disagreement; batching and protected blocks (bench rung 4: 4.6 h of resuming saved); the 40 percent and 23-minute claims tiered. Build-along: an interruption log and a batching rule.
6. **Saying no, and the plan you keep** - handing back and renegotiating (rung 3: 36 asks handed back); recovery (F18 read, F19 reported); demand-control (F20); a weekly plan with a slack line. Build-along: the weekly plan page and a renegotiation script.
