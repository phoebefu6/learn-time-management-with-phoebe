Time and stress management as an operating skill: protect your attention and energy by planning against the capacity you really have. What is honest here 🧾

Productivity advice travels with famous numbers, and several of the most quoted ones do not come from where they are said to come from. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Every bench number: real load, waits, deadlines missed, overtime, backlogModelled. A seeded queue of Dara's constructed work, run minute by minute in your browser and labelled on every number; an independent Python run reproduces every default value exactly
The queueing curve: waiting grows as utilisation rises, slowly then steeplyTheory, checked. Kingman's approximation, read in university lecture notes, drawn next to a long simulation of the same task mix; they agree within 0.4 hours of waiting up to 91 percent load
"Students forecast 33.9 days for a thesis that took 55.5"Read at source in Buehler, Griffin and Ross (1994)
"Eisenhower's urgent-important matrix"Half true. In 1954 Eisenhower quoted an unnamed college president on urgent and important problems; the four-quadrant matrix is Stephen Covey's, decades later (reported)
"Multitasking costs 40 percent of productive time" and "23 minutes to refocus"Not what the papers say. The 40 percent is an estimate quoted by the APA, not a result of the 2001 experiments; 23 minutes could not be traced to a paper, and the 2005 field study reports 25 minutes 26 seconds to resume, with a huge spread.
